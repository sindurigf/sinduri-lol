# Voice

How editorial copy sounds: posts, About, Career, Contact, taglines and anything
else in the first person. Functional copy follows
[AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md#copy).

## Tone

- Fun, cheeky, sassy, warm and direct. Write like a real person, not a company.
- Warm and positive without going over the top.
- Humble: credit other people and avoid self-promotion.
- Humor carries a fact. A joke with nothing true inside it is reworded until
  it has one.
- Concrete details over adjectives: Minerva sips wine and turns her nose up at
  meat, not "a sweet cat".
- Values said plainly: "Being only critical makes you a jerk, not an honest
  person."
- Short lines for the things that matter.
- Generous about people; own the embarrassing bits.
- Thanks are specific: say what the person did.
- Bios and taglines say in simple terms what the maintainer does and cares
  about: "Open Source Enthusiast, Positivity Advocate, Continuous Learner", not
  "results-driven professional".

## Words

- Plain, everyday words by default.
- Big or emotional words ("incredible", "journey", "adventure") only where they
  truly fit, never as filler.
- No word repeated through a piece, and no two sentences in a row starting the
  same way.
- No forced metaphors or jargon ("on-ramps").
- Every sentence makes clear what it refers to.
- Honest about where things stand: no overstated results or momentum.

## Never

- Lines that sound clever and say nothing ("Good incentives beat good
  intentions every time").
- Marketing, corporate or press-release language ("We are thrilled to announce
  an exciting opportunity").
- Slogans and overdone taglines ("Mission-driven. Community-powered. Open by
  design.").
- Dramatic setups ("The shift I want to offer is this") and closers that try
  too hard ("People stay for other people, not for a repository").
- Flippant asides, filler comparisons, rankings the maintainer did not make.
- Stock phrasing the maintainer does not use, such as "hooked".
- Swear words.
- Search or meta phrasing lifted into visible copy.

## Grammar

| Rule        | Write                                                         |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Spelling    | US English: organize, recognize, behavior, favorite           |
| Headings    | Chicago title case: "People and Places", "What AI Changes"    |
| Dashes      | None as punctuation: a comma, a full stop or a new sentence   |
| Apostrophes | No negative contractions ("do not"); "that's" is fine         |
| Lists       | No comma before the last "and": "wine, watermelon and cake"   |
| Numbers     | Words under 101 ("five maintainers"); figures for dates and % |
| Quotes      | Song and talk titles in quotation marks; punctuation outside  |
| Names       | Brands in their own casing: mossbo, drunomics                 |

Negative contractions are written out because many readers find them harder
to read, or misread them as the opposite
([GOV.UK](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/style-guides/a-to-z-style-guide/)).

## Method

- Drafts use only the maintainer's own words and facts: posts, other writing and
  answers to questions. No invented details, dates, contact details or claims.
- No fact for a line: ask the maintainer, never fill the gap.
- Edit, do not rewrite: change only what is needed, fix spelling and grammar,
  split long sentences, cut repeats, keep the original words and tone.
- Improve, do not remove: a flippant or unclear line is reworded, never cut,
  and no fact is lost.
- Bullets where content is a list; prose where it is an argument or a story.
- No formatting or styling the maintainer did not ask for.
- Feedback is applied as ideas, not copied word for word.
- Show two to five numbered options with one recommendation, and say what
  changed; the maintainer approves every line.
- Keep the whole page cohesive: headings, leads, captions and closers read as
  one set.
