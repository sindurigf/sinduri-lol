# Voice

How editorial copy sounds: posts, About, Career, Contact, taglines, anything in
Sinduri's voice. Functional copy follows [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md#copy).

## Tone

- Fun, cheeky, sassy, warm and direct.
- Humour carries a fact. A joke with nothing true inside it is cut.
- Concrete details over adjectives: Minerva sips wine and turns her nose up at
  meat, not "a sweet cat".
- Values said plainly: "Being only critical makes you a jerk, not an honest
  person."
- Short lines for the things that matter.
- Generous about people; own the embarrassing bits.

## Never

- Lines that sound clever and say nothing ("Good incentives beat good
  intentions every time").
- Flippant asides, filler comparisons, rankings she did not make.
- Stock phrasing she does not use, such as "hooked".
- Swear words.
- Search or meta phrasing lifted into visible copy.

## Grammar

| Rule        | Write                                                         |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Spelling    | UK English: organise, recognise, behaviour, favourite         |
| Headings    | Chicago title case: "People and Places", "What AI Changes"    |
| Apostrophes | No contractions ("I do not"); possessives only ("the year's") |
| Lists       | No comma before the last "and": "wine, watermelon and cake"   |
| Numbers     | Words under 101 ("five maintainers"); figures for dates and % |
| Quotes      | Song and talk titles in quotation marks; punctuation outside  |

## Method

- Drafts use only Sinduri's own words and facts: her writing, her posts, her
  answers. No invented anecdotes, feelings or claims.
- No fact for a line: ask her, never fill the gap.
- Edit, do not rewrite: fix spelling and grammar, split long sentences, cut
  repeats, keep her words.
- Improve, do not remove: a flippant or unclear line is reworded, never cut,
  and no fact is lost.
- Bullets where content is a list; prose where it is an argument or a story.
- Show numbered options with one recommendation; she approves every line.
- Keep the whole page cohesive: headings, leads, captions and closers read as
  one set.
